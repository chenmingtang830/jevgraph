# Design Health Score

24/40 before revision. The evidence was accurate, but the long-form page made the primary result
harder to find than the methodology.

## Design specificity verdict

The Proofpress palette and rules were recognizable, but the composition behaved like a generic
long-form landing page rather than a benchmark brief. The product-specific opportunity was the
three-way operational tradeoff, and it did not appear as one immediate visual comparison.

## Priority issues

1. **P1 - Results below narrative:** the reader had to process a large hero and explanatory prose
   before seeing a comparison. Fix: put latency, cost, and accuracy bars together on page one.
2. **P1 - Excessive reading load:** repeated explanations of scope, cost basis, and readiness
   obscured the measured result. Fix: compress to two pages and move all caveats beside the data.
3. **P2 - Interaction without payoff:** switching one metric at a time hid two thirds of the result.
   Fix: show all three charts simultaneously; reserve interaction for genuinely deeper inspection.

## Persona red flags

- A first-time open-source evaluator could not answer “what won and what was traded off?” within
  one glance.
- A technical reviewer had to reconcile repeated prose instead of scanning one comparable figure.

## Resolution

Replaced the long report with a two-page benchmark brief. Page one contains three aligned bar
charts and the derived Jev advantages. Page two contains only the task contract, failure ledger,
readiness judgment, and limitations.
